1. What is this system?

Abstract IMS (Inventory Management System) is a web application made for a medicine shop / pharmacy. It replaces paper registers and scattered spreadsheets. You open it in a web browser (it can also be installed on a phone or computer like an app) and sign in with your email and password.

In one sentence

Whatever medicine comes in, goes out, expires, or is paid for — the system remembers it and tells you the result.

What can you do with it?

  • Keep a list of all your medicines and know how many are in stock at any time.
  • Track every batch of every medicine with its expiry date, cost price and selling price.
  • Record purchases from suppliers — stock goes up automatically.
  • Record sales to customers — stock goes down automatically, and you can print a tax invoice.
  • Get warned early about medicines that are expiring soon, already expired, or running low.
  • Send expired stock back to the supplier, or write it off as a loss.
  • Know who owes you and whom you owe with a full account statement (ledger) for every supplier and customer.
  • Record shop expenses (rent, electricity, salary, etc.).
  • See how your business is doing with a Dashboard and Analytics: sales, expenses and profit.
  • Add your staff with their own logins.
Made for NepalAmounts are in Rs. (Rupees), VAT defaults to 13%, PAN/VAT numbers are supported, and the Party Ledger shows both Nepali (BS) and English dates with the fiscal year.

The most important idea: Product vs. Batch

This is the one idea worth understanding, because the rest of the system follows from it.

  • A Product (Item) is the kind of medicine. Example: “Cefixime 200 mg”. You create it once.
  • A Batch is one specific delivery of that product, with its own batch number, expiry date, buying price and selling price. Example: “Cefixime 200 mg — Batch AB2501, expires Dec 2027, 100 strips”.

Why? Because two boxes of the same medicine can expire on different dates and cost different amounts. The system keeps them separate so that nothing is mixed up and you always know exactly which stock is about to expire.

One product, many batches

PRODUCTCefixime 200 mg
→
Batch AB2501 exp. Dec 2027 · 100 pcs
Batch AB2412 exp. in 40 days · 25 pcs
Batch AB2310 EXPIRED · 10 pcs
The product's total stock is the sum of its usable batches.
Smart selling: earliest expiry firstWhen you sell, the batch list shows the batch with the earliest expiry date first, so older stock is sold before newer stock. Expired batches cannot be sold — the system blocks them.

6. Page-by-page guide

Below, every page is explained in the same way: what it is for, what you see, and what you can do.

6.1 Dashboard

Menu: Dashboard  ·  /pharma

Purpose: Your shop's health at a glance. It is the first page you see after login.

What you see

  • Date filter — choose Monthly (from month – to month) or Yearly (from year – to year). A Reset button brings back the default (this year so far).
  • Four summary cards
    • Total Sales — money your sales brought in during the chosen period.
    • Net Profit — total sales minus purchases and other expenses (with the profit margin %).
    • Purchases & Outflow — money spent on buying stock plus other expenses.
    • Inventory Status — total items, how many are low on stock, and how many are expiring.
  • Revenue, Outflows & Profit Trends — a chart that compares revenue, spending and profit over time.
  • Revenue Breakdown — a pie chart showing where revenue comes from. Switch between by Category and by Payment type.
  • Critical Stock & Expiry Watchlist — a short list of medicines that need attention: Expired Expiring Soon (within 15 days) and Low Stock. It shows 5 per page.
  • Recent Transactions — the latest sales, purchases and expenses.

What you can do

  • Change the date range to study a different period.
  • Press Download Report on the watchlist to open a ready-made Critical Stock Report. You can choose what to include, then Export to Excel/CSV or Print / Save as PDF. It also has a note for physical stock verification (counting what is really on the shelf).

6.2 Inventory

Menu: Operations → Inventory  ·  /pharma/inventory

Purpose: The master list of all your medicines (items) and how much of each you have.

What you see

  • Four cards: Total Items, In Stock, Low Stock, Out of Stock.
  • Search box — find by medicine name, brand, category or HSN code.
  • Filters — by category, and by stock level (All / In Stock / Low Stock / Out of Stock).
  • Table columns: Item & Brand • Category • HSN Code • Unit • Total Stock • Number of Batches • Low-stock Threshold • Status • Actions.
  • Click an item to open its batches: batch no., current stock, expiry date, manufacturing date, purchase rate, M.R.P., sale price, supplier and status. You can also click a batch to read its notes.

What you can do

  • Add an item — fill in the name (e.g. “Cefixime 200 MG”), a short name, HSN code, category, unit (Pcs, Strip, Box…), the low-stock limit, and a short description.
  • Edit or delete an item.
  • Manage categories — add or delete your own categories (e.g. Antibiotics, Syrups).
NoteYou do not type stock numbers here. Stock goes up when you record a Purchase and down when you record a Sale. This keeps your numbers honest.

6.3 Batches

Menu: Operations → Batches  ·  /pharma/batches

Purpose: Keep an eye on expiry dates and deal with expired stock (return it or write it off).

What you see

  • Cards: Total Batches, Active Batches, Expired Batches.
  • Search by batch, product or supplier.
  • Tabs: All • Expired • Near Expiry • Safe. Another filter shows only batches that need action, have a pending return, a completed return, or were written off.
  • Table columns: Batch & Product • Supplier • Expiry Date • Countdown (days left) • Available Qty • Purchase Price • Value at Risk (money locked in that stock) • Status • Actions.

What you can do

  • Edit a batch — change its note, sale price, dates and similar details.
  • Return to supplier — enter the quantity and the reason (e.g. “expired”, “damaged”). The stock is taken off your shelf and the return is marked Pending.
  • Complete the return when the supplier settles it, in one of two ways:
    • Credit Note / Refund — you get money or a deduction (you enter the amount).
    • Replacement Stock — the supplier gives new stock, which is added as a new batch.
    Then the return becomes Completed.
  • Write off an expired batch — the remaining quantity is removed from stock, and its cost is saved as a loss under the expense category “Inventory Write-off”. You can add a reason (e.g. “Expired on shelf”).

6.4 Purchase

Menu: Operations → Purchase  ·  /pharma/purchase

Purpose: Record the medicines you buy from suppliers. This is how new stock enters the system.

What you see

  • Cards: Total Purchases, Total Spend, Cash Purchases, Credit Purchases.
  • Search and filters: search by invoice, supplier, medicine or batch; filter by payment type (Cash / Credit / Bank Transfer / Cheque / Mobile Payment), payment status (Paid / Partial / Unpaid), party, and a date range.
  • Table columns: Date • Invoice No. • Payment • Status • Purchase Type • Party • Items & Batches • Amount • Actions.

The “New Purchase” form has 4 parts

  1. Purchase Voucher Details — supplier's invoice number, date, payment type, and VAT type: VAT/Exempt (no VAT), VAT/Item-wise (VAT only on selected items), or VAT/Tax Included (prices already include VAT).
  2. Party — search for the supplier, or press Add to create a new supplier right there without leaving the page.
  3. Items & Batch Details — for each medicine: item, quantity, unit, purchase rate, then batch number and expiry date (required), manufacturing date, M.R.P., sale price and an optional note.
  4. Discounts & Charges — discount, freight & forwarding charges, rounding off, VAT refund. Each can be a flat amount (Rs.) or a percentage. You also get a purchase note box. The totals are shown, and the final figures are confirmed when you save.

What happens when you save

  • A new batch is created for each item and the stock goes up.
  • The purchase amount is also recorded as an “Inventory” expense, so your profit figures stay correct.

Other things you can do

  • Change payment status — mark Paid / Partial / Unpaid. For a part payment, a small window asks for the amount, method (Cash, Bank, Mobile Payment, Cheque), a cheque/transaction number and a note. It shows the Total Bill and the Balance Due.
  • Print / Download the purchase voucher.
  • Edit a purchase.
  • Delete a purchase (stock from it is taken back out). This is blocked if some of that stock has already been sold, to keep your records correct.

6.5 Sales

Menu: Operations → Sales  ·  /pharma/sales

Purpose: Record every sale, take payment, and print the invoice.

What you see

  • Cards: Total Sales, Total Revenue, Cash Sales, Credit Sales.
  • Search and filters: search by invoice, party or note; filter by payment type, payment status and party (including “Walk-in (Cash)”).
  • Table columns: Date • Invoice No. • Payment • Status • Party • Items & Dispensed Batches • Total Qty • Amount • Actions.

The “New Sale” form has 4 parts

  1. Voucher & Party — date, payment type (Cash / Credit / Bank Transfer / Cheque / Mobile Payment), payment status, and the customer. Choose Walk-in / Cash Customer if you don't need to register them, pick an existing party, or add a new one on the spot. The invoice number is given automatically, in order, with no gaps.
  2. Items & Batch Allocation — search the medicine, choose the batch (the one expiring first is highlighted as “Earliest Expiry”), type the quantity and sale rate. The system warns you if you try to sell more than the batch holds.
  3. Discounts & Charges — discount, freight, rounding off, VAT refund (flat or %).
  4. Notes / Prescription Reference — type the doctor's name or any dispensing remarks.

A running summary shows Subtotal, Discount, Freight, VAT refund, estimated VAT (13% by default; each item can be marked VAT-applicable or not) and the estimated Grand Total. The final totals are calculated by the system when you save.

What happens when you save

  • Stock is reduced from the batches you selected.
  • Expired batches and out-of-stock quantities are rejected with a clear message.

Other things you can do

  • Print / Save as PDF a Tax Invoice — it shows your business details, customer, item list with batch and expiry, discount, taxable/non-taxable amounts, grand amount in words, bank details and signature lines (Delivered By / Received By / Checked By).
  • Change payment status (Paid / Partial / Unpaid), with the same part-payment window as in Purchase.
  • Edit a sale.
  • Delete a sale — the system asks you to confirm: “This will reverse the stock deducted by this sale.”

7. Everyday workflows, step by step

A. Setting up for the first time

1. Settingsbusiness name, logo, PAN/VAT
→
2. Partiesadd suppliers & customers
→
3. Inventoryadd categories & items
→
4. Purchaseenter opening stock
→
5. Add Accountinvite staff
Opening stock is entered as a Purchase so that every unit has a batch and an expiry date.

B. Buying new stock

  1. Go to Purchase and press the button to add a new purchase.
  2. Enter the supplier's invoice number, date, payment type and VAT type.
  3. Pick the supplier (or add a new one).
  4. Add each medicine with quantity, rate, batch number and expiry date (required).
  5. Add any discount or freight, check the totals, and Save.
  6. The stock is now live in Inventory and Batches. If you haven't paid yet, leave the status as Unpaid and update it later.

C. Selling medicine

  1. Go to Sales and press the button to add a new sale.
  2. Choose Walk-in, or pick / add the customer. Choose payment type and status.
  3. Search each medicine, pick the batch (earliest expiry first) and enter the quantity.
  4. Add a discount if any, write the prescription note if needed, and Save.
  5. Press the print icon on the sale to produce the Tax Invoice (print or save as PDF).
  6. If the customer pays later, open the sale and change its status to Partial or Paid.

D. Handling expiring and expired medicine

  1. Check the Dashboard watchlist regularly — or open Batches and use the Near Expiry tab.
  2. Sell near-expiry batches first.
  3. For batches that have expired, open the batch and choose either:
    • Return to supplier, then later mark it complete as Credit Note / Refund or Replacement Stock; or
    • Write off the stock (recorded as a loss).

E. Paying a supplier or collecting money from a customer

  1. Open Purchase (for suppliers) or Sales (for customers) and find the bill.
  2. Change the status to Partial or Paid.
  3. For a part-payment, enter the amount, payment method and any cheque / transaction number.
  4. Open Party Ledger to see the updated balance for that party.

F. Reviewing your business at month end

  1. Record any remaining Expenses.
  2. Open Dashboard for the quick view, or Analytics for the detailed charts.
  3. Download the Critical Stock Report and count the shelf stock against it.
  4. Print Party Ledgers to settle accounts with suppliers and customers.

10. Safety & good habits

  • Your data belongs only to your business. Every login is tied to one business, and other businesses cannot see your records.
  • Sign-in is protected. Wrong passwords are limited to 5 attempts, and passwords are stored in scrambled form, never in plain text.
  • Use a strong password (at least 8 characters) and don't share it. Give each staff member their own account.
  • Always log out on shared computers.
  • Enter purchases and sales on the same day. The numbers in Dashboard, Analytics and Ledger are only as good as the entries behind them.
  • Enter correct expiry dates. The warnings and the “cannot sell expired” protection rely on them.
  • Set a low-stock limit for important medicines so you are warned before you run out.
  • Count your shelf now and then and compare with the Critical Stock Report.
  • Deleting or editing a bill changes your stock. Do it only when the entry was a mistake.

11. Questions & answers

Do I type the stock quantity myself?

No. Stock changes only through Purchases (up), Sales (down), Returns and Write-offs (down). That way there is always a record of why the number changed.

I cannot find the batch I want when selling.

Only batches that still have stock and have not expired are shown. Check the Batches page to see the batch's status and quantity.

The system says I cannot delete a purchase.

Some stock from that purchase has already been sold or moved. To correct it, edit the purchase instead, or use a return/write-off for the remaining stock.

A customer is not registered. Can I still sell?

Yes. Choose Walk-in / Cash Customer in the sale form.

What is the difference between “Return to supplier” and “Write-off”?

Return means the supplier takes the goods back and gives you money or new stock. Write-off means the stock is thrown away, and its cost is counted as your loss.

Where do I change VAT?

VAT is set in each purchase (by VAT type) and in each sale (13% by default, and each item can be marked VAT-applicable or not). Your business PAN/VAT number is in Settings → General.

I forgot my password.

Use Forgot password? on the Login page. A 6-digit code is sent to your email.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes. The pages adapt to the phone screen, and the system can be installed to the home screen like an app. An internet connection is needed to work with your data.

Who do I contact for help?

Please contact your system provider with a short description (and a screenshot, if possible) of what you were doing and what you saw on the screen.
